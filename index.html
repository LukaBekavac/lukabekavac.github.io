I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of St. Gallen, in the Interactions group led by Prof. Dr. Simon Mayer.

I focus on understanding and addressing the systemic risks posed by Very Large Online Platforms. I combine methods from computer science, technology law, and the social sciences to study how platforms' personalized recommender systems influence us, while developing tools to enhance transparency and accountability in their operation.

Outside research: hiking, chess, and padel.

Publications

  • Scrutinizing Systemic Risks in Personalized Recommender Systems Through Sock-Puppet Auditing of VLOPs L. Bekavac, J. Strecker-Bischoff, K. Garcia, S. Mayer, A. Tamò-Larrieux ACM Transactions on Recommender Systems, 2026

    Introduces SOAP, an open-source sock-puppet auditing framework that reaches the user-level granularity platform data access does not provide. Applied to TikTok during the 2024 German regional elections, it found that politically neutral accounts were three to four times more likely to be shown right-wing populist content than mainstream party content.

  • Platforms' Research API Data Access: What Users See vs. What Researchers Can Retrieve L. Bekavac, S. Mayer ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (FAccT), 2026

    Compares what the TikTok Research API and Meta Content Library return against what is actually publicly visible on those platforms, using controlled accounts during election periods. Three classes of platform-imposed loss emerge — scope narrowing, metadata stripping, and operational restriction — removing roughly half of visible content and up to 83% of contextual metadata, which falls short of supporting independent auditing of systemic risks under Article 40(12) DSA.

    DOI arXiv
  • Broken Links and Fading Stories: How Dominant Online Platforms Subvert the Hypermedia Foundation of the Web L. Bekavac, S. Mayer 18th ACM Web Science Conference (WebSci), 2026

    The Web was envisioned as an open, decentralized network of documents joined by hyperlinks. Dominant platforms depart from that architecture — constraining linking, limiting navigation, and normalizing ephemerality — turning an open hypermedia environment into platform-governed silos. Users answer with transcension practices (reposts, screenshots, off-platform link hubs), but these are ad-hoc workarounds; the paper argues for reclaiming the hyperlink as a first-class architectural primitive.

    DOI
  • From Data Portability to Data Control: Making Regulation Technically Real L. Bekavac Poster, 4th Solid Symposium, London, 2026

    GDPR Art. 20 and DMA Art. 6(9) mandate social media data portability, but platform exports arrive as proprietary dumps with no semantic structure — technically compliant, practically useless for switching. Presents an end-to-end pipeline that uses LLM-assisted RDF conversion to turn those exports into structured linked data in user-controlled Solid Pods, enabling import into decentralized services such as Bluesky.

    Zenodo Poster
  • A Solid Research Infrastructure Leveraging DMA Data Portability L. K. Seiling, J. Stein, S. Mayer, L. Bekavac 2026

    Proposes a shared research infrastructure that turns the data-portability rights created by the Digital Markets Act into a usable basis for studying platforms, with exported data held in Solid pods.

    OpenReview
  • Connecting Personalized Realities: Challenges and Opportunities in a Personalized Society J. Strecker-Bischoff, L. Bekavac, S. Mayer, K. Bektas 1st Workshop on Shaping Future Human Connection: Social Augmentation through XR Technologies, at ACM CHI 2026

    XR and AI increasingly mediate how people perceive and navigate reality. Such Personalized Realities can make interaction more effective and information more equitable, but they also risk isolating people in perceptual filter bubbles and eroding shared understanding. Introduces the term Personalized Society, maps the tensions it creates, and asks how far frameworks like the Digital Services Act can protect individual and societal rights within one.

    PDF
  • Change Your Perspective, Widen Your Worldview! Societally Beneficial Perceptual Filter Bubbles in Personalized Reality J. Strecker, L. Bekavac, K. Bektaş, S. Mayer arXiv preprint, 2025

    Argues that Extended Reality risks producing perceptual filter bubbles, exposing people predominantly to content congruent with their existing beliefs and narrowing the worldview they can even perceive. Proposes carrying mitigations already shown to work in recommender systems — perspective awareness, engineered serendipity, and support for self-actualization — into XR, so people keep transparency and meaningful control over their personalized reality.

    arXiv
  • Visualizing Children's Filter Bubbles: A Digital Window for Bridging Social Media Experiences L. Bekavac, L. Bekavac, B. Bekavac MIPRO 2025 — 48th ICT and Electronics Convention, Opatija

    Children are still forming their critical thinking, so the filter bubbles that recommender systems build around them shape worldviews in ways parents rarely see. Uses SOAP to steer and replicate the feeds children actually experience, then presents an interactive interface that visualizes those bubbles for parents. The aim is to make generational differences in digital experience comparable, and to give families a concrete starting point for talking about algorithmic personalization.

    DOI
  • QR-Code Integrity by Design L. Bekavac, S. Mayer, J. Strecker-Bischoff CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, Extended Abstracts, 2024

    QR codes are everywhere, which makes tampering with them — quishing — a real threat. Introduces SafeQR codes, which use visual design and secure design principles to make tampering visible rather than relying on user education alone. A user study shows these "integrity by design" codes measurably raise suspicion when tampered with and reduce successful quishing attacks.

    DOI PDF
  • From Walls to Windows: Creating Transparency to Understand Filter Bubbles in Social Media L. Bekavac, K. Garcia, J. Strecker-Bischoff, S. Mayer, A. Tamò-Larrieux NORMalize 2024 — 2nd Workshop on the Normative Design and Evaluation of Recommender Systems, at ACM RecSys 2024

    Filter bubbles have been hard to study at scale despite years of research. Introduces SOAP, which takes a topic prompt and uses a multimodal LLM to create and navigate filter bubbles inside a real feed, logging what it sees. Across three induced bubbles, topic diversity collapsed in as little as 60 minutes of scrolling.

    PDF Code
  • Towards an Integrated Semantic Approach to GHG Accounting L. Bekavac, L. Volk University of St. Gallen, Integrative Master Project, 2024

    Switzerland's incoming reporting rules require comprehensive greenhouse-gas accounting, but existing formats and methodologies do not interoperate. Proposes an ontology derived from life-cycle-assessment terminology as a unifying layer, combined with linked-data distribution over Solid Pods for granular access control and real-time updates.
